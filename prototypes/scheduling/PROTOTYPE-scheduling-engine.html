Does the whole planner reduce to one scheduling function?

The claim being tested: laying a Course onto a Class's timetable needs no move, shift or insert operations. There is one function — hand it the Lessons in order and the Available Slots in order, and it zips them together — plus a little state. Every disruption is then just a change to its input, and the answer is recomputed from scratch.

Press the buttons and try to break it. Everything below runs against the real 2026/27 calendar — the six Terms, the INSET days, the stored Week A/B letters. The timetable is representative, not Ed's actual one: 9B/Sc1 gets five periods a fortnight including a Thursday double, 10C/Ph2 gets two.

Watch the boundary. The heavy line in the schedule is today. Above it is the record of what happened; below it is plan. The function may only write below the line — that one rule is what stops re-running from rewriting the past.
